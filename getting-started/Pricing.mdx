### How card pricing works: Interchange++

Every card payment consists of three components, together known as the **Interchange++ (IC++) model**:

**1. Interchange fee**  
The interchange fee is paid to the bank that issued your customer’s card. It compensates the bank for handling the transaction and carrying the risk of the payment.

Within the European Economic Area (EEA), interchange fees are capped by law:  
- Up to **0.3%** of the transaction amount for credit cards  
- Up to **0.2%** of the transaction amount for debit cards  

The actual fee depends on the card type (debit, credit, corporate, premium) and whether the card is domestic or international.  
Domestic debit cards are usually the lowest cost, while international or corporate credit cards tend to be higher.

For the most recent details, you can refer to:  
<tip>[Visa interchange fees](https://www.visa.com)  </tip>
[Mastercard interchange fees](https://www.mastercard.com)
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField>
**2. Scheme fee**  
The scheme fee goes to the card network (Visa, Mastercard, etc.) and covers the cost of running the global payment infrastructure.

**3. Processor markup**  
This is the **Checky Pro fee**. It allows us to provide the technology, fraud protection, and support required to process your transactions securely.

With **Interchange++**, you can clearly see how the total cost of each card payment is structured.  
It is not a hidden “all-in” number, but a transparent breakdown of the real underlying costs plus the Checky Pro service fee.
</ResponseField>
